Lead time = how long BEFORE the cliff (margin 0 dB, packet loss starts) each detector alarms. Higher is better; negative = alarm after the loss started.

Generated by `python analysis/detection_study.py`. Monitor noise sigma = 0.15 dB (the optical agent's). False alarms are averaged over three simulated 24 h days.

## What the numbers say

1. **No single detector wins everywhere.** The slope-based trend gives the most warning for fast and medium degradations (2.6 to 12 min before the cliff) but **misses very slow ones** (0.1 dB/min), whose slope stays below its threshold; there the plain 3 dB threshold warns 31.6 min ahead.
2. **That is why the lab runs both** (`FtOsnrMarginLow` OR `FtOsnrMarginTrend`): the combination never misses and always takes the earlier of the two.
3. **On our metro links (22-29 dB of margin) false alarms are not the problem**: noise, glitches and re-patch steps never push any detector over. Lead time is what matters.
4. **On a tight link (4 dB of margin) a fixed threshold is unusable as an alarm** (dozens per day): the threshold should be relative to the design margin ("lost X dB"), not absolute.
5. **Theil-Sen matches the least-squares trend on lead time with fewer false alarms** (it ignores single bad readings). Recommendation: compute the slope with Theil-Sen in the exporter for production.
6. **CUSUM, with textbook tuning, never false-alarms but only catches fast changes**; tuned for slow drifts it would need a smaller k, trading false alarms. It is the right tool for sudden steps, not for aging.

Limits: slower degradations and the healthy days are simulated with the same optical model and noise as the agent (labelled as such); only the 6 dB/min case is a lab recording.
